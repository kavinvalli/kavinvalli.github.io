This is a draft. I'm thinking out loud.

Most of what I ship starts as a bigger idea that I whittle down until it fits in
an afternoon. Not because the bigger idea is wrong — usually it's fine — but
because the smaller version is the one that actually reaches the world.

## What "small" buys me

- **A real ending.** A small thing has a finish line I can see from the start.
- **Feedback that isn't hypothetical.** People react to what exists, not to what
  I've described.
- **Permission to be wrong.** If I built it in a day, I can throw it out in a
  day.

## Where it breaks

Small ships badly when the problem has a floor — infra, correctness, anything
where 80% done is 0% useful. In those cases the "small" move is usually to pick
a smaller *slice of the problem*, not a smaller version of the solution.
